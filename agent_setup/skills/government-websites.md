# Finding official Indian government sources

- Official websites of Indian government bodies are usually on **.gov.in** or **.nic.in**,
  often as a subdomain of the state portal (e.g. `<agency>.<state>.gov.in`). Some older
  parastatals still use **.org**, **.in** or **.co.in** domains; treat these as candidates to
  verify, not as official by default.
- The **state urban development department** website and the **state portal** usually list
  the parastatals under the department and link to their websites. These pages are good
  evidence of which website is genuine.
- Many government pages are slow, need JavaScript, or show content only after a click. Prefer
  direct links to documents (PDFs) and specific pages over home pages.
- Statutory disclosures are often grouped under menus such as "RTI" / "Proactive Disclosure"
  (Section 4(1)(b) of the RTI Act), "Finance", "Accounts", "Reports", "Tenders", "Acts &
  Rules" or "Citizen Charter".
- Budgets of state parastatals may also appear in the **state budget documents** published by
  the finance department.
- Never treat aggregators, news sites, Wikipedia, directories or social media as official
  sources. They can point you to an official document, but cite the official document itself.
