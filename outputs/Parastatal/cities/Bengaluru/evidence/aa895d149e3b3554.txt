Definitions 
 
a. On-Street Parking- available parking spaces for vehicles on a street along with the 
sidewalk, or anywhere on the street at available/ designated parking spaces for 
vehicles on a street. 
b. Off-Street Parking- Off-street parking refers to available parking spaces for vehicles 
within an enclosed parking lot or garage. Parking lots may be owned by a 
Municipality, Municipal Corporation/Government organizations or privately owned. 
c. Parking Zones/areas - a space where any kind of automobile can be parked. 
d. Parking Lot – A designated place where one can  leave their private 
vehicles/automobile for a specific period of time. 
e. Terminal Stations - The first and last stations on a  Metro corridor. 
f. Interchange Metro stations – Interchange Metro Station  is a Metro station that 
serves as a connecting point between two or more Metro lines or different modes 
of public transportation . It allows passengers to transfer from one line to another 
without exiting the station premises, maki ng their journey smoother and more 
efficient 
g. Metro – In this present context, Metro shall mean BMRCL premises/network. 
h. Mobility Management  - Mobility management can be broadly defined as  creating and 
managing mobility options, at both the systemic and system-to-customer levels, to improve 
the reach, efficiency, and affordability of public transportation services. 
i. CBD - Central Business District (CBD)  is the commercial and business hub of a city , 
characterized by high -density development, tall buildings and a concentration of offices, 
retail outlets, financial institutions and services. It is often the most accessible part of the 
city, served by multiple public transportation modes. 
j. ITS - Intelligent Transportation System (ITS)  refers to the application of advanced 
information and communication technologies (ICT) to enhance the efficiency, safety, and 
sustainability of transportation systems. ITS improves traffic management, provides real -
time information, and enhances the overa ll mobility experience for commuters and 
operators. 
k. First and Last mile connectivity - The first and last stage of an individual trip – from their 
starting point to the nearest public transport station (first mile) and from the last station to 

[page 4]
 pg. 2 
their final d estination (last mile). Effective first and last mile solutions are crucial for 
ensuring seamless, convenient, and efficient public transportation. 
l. Micro-Mobility Vehicles- Electric vehicles with a maximum speed of 25 kilometres per hour 
(kmph) and its equipped motor shall have a thirty-minute power less than 250 watt. These 
vehicles are approved by any authorized testing agency specified in the rule 126 of the 
Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989 as being compliant with the requirements specified in 
Notification no. G.S.R. 291(E) (2) dated 24.04.2014 of the Government of India. 
m. Transit Stations- Bus terminals, Metro stations, Railway stations and large transportation 
hubs like Traffic Transit Management Centres, Multi-modal hubs that serve as a gateway 
to access public transport operations. 
n. Park and Ride- - a designated parking area near public transportation hubs, such as Metro 
stations, bus terminals, or rail stations, where commuters can park their private vehicles 
(cars, bikes, or bicycles) and transfer to public transit for the remainder of their journey.  
o. Urban Gateway - An Urban gateway is a key access point that connects a city to 
surrounding regions or the wider world through major transportation infrastructure like 
airports, railways, and highways. It facilitates the flow of people and goods, playing a crucial 
role in the City’s economic and social connectivity.

BMRCL METRO PARKING POLICY FOR BENGALURU CITY 
1. Background 
Bengaluru is the capital city of Karnataka and  is one of the fastes t growing Cities in the 
Country. With the exponential increase in population of Bengaluru coupled with a rapid 
increase in the income level, there has been a consequent upsurge in number of registered 
vehicles plying in the City.  
According to the data published by the Transport Department, Government of Karnataka, 
on an average 2500 number of vehicles are being registered in RTO each day, the number 
of registered vehicles in the city as on March 2024 has crossed 1 crore (10million) and the 
compounded annual growth rate of vehicle registration is over 10% per annum. 
According to a report by TomTom, the Netherlands-based global provider of navigation, 
traffic and map products, Bengaluru ranks a low 149 (of 231), on the 2019 Mercer’s Quality 
of Living Index, it is one of the world's most traffic-congested city. Average speed on critical 
roads drop to less than 10 km per hour during peak traffic hours  and travel time are 48% 
longer than during the baseline non -congested conditions. This enormous trend of rise in 
numbers of vehicles is creating pressur e on the parking needs of the City which is in turn 
affects the demand for parking at Metro stations as well.  
Bangalore Metro, also known as Namma Metro, is a Mass Rapid Transit System (MRTS) that 
has transformed the way people commute in Bengaluru. Bengaluru, often referred to as 
the Silicon Valley of India, is known for its traffic congestion. The Metro Rail system has 
provided a much needed respite to the city's residents by reducing the number of vehicles 
on the roads and by reducing the pollution caused by the traffic congestion. 
The total sanctioned network of BMRCL of Phase -1, Phase -2, Phase -2A, Phase -2B and  
Phase-3 is about 220.20 Km. Bangalore Metro Rail Network of Phase-1 and part of Phase-2 
comprising a total length of 96.1 Km with 83 Metro stations is already under operation  with 
an average daily ridership of about 9.5 lakhs commuters. The remaining portion of Phase -
2 of 21.26 Km length is under construction. In addition, the Phase-2A Metro corridor from 
Central Silk Board (CSB) to K R Puram along the Outer Ring Road for a route length of 19.75 
kms with 13 Elevated Stations and the Phase -2B Metro corridor from K R Puram to 
Kempegowda International Airport (KIA) for a route length of 38.44 kms with 16 Elevated 
+1 underground stations  are under construction  and Phase 3 Metro corridor of a route 
length 44.65 kms with 31 stations is under construction. With the increase in the patronage

of Metro commuters, the demand in parking at the Metro stations has also increased 
substantially. BMRCL has attempted to provide parking  space at its Metro stations so that

present, BMRCL has a total area of 1.15 lakh sq.m area of parking space at 69 Metro stations

2. Parking Policy 2.0 for Bengaluru City 
The Government of Karnataka has approved Parking Policy 2.0 for Bengaluru and the 
Directorate of Urban Land Transport (DULT) has prepared the Area Parking Plans for all 
zones in Bengaluru (BBMP Area) in accordance with the said parking policy. The overarching 
principle of the Parking Policy 2.0 emphasises on reducing  the d emand for parking and 
facilitating organized on-street parking so that the road space can be efficiently utilised for 
transit and NMT supportive uses. The policy suggested paid parking to discourage the use 
of private mode and thereby ensuring modal shift to public transport, development of off-
street parking facilities through market driven parking supply and management mechanism 
and enforcing strict regulations to manage the parking demand. 
Further, the Parking P olicy 2.0 has also recommended  park-and-ride (off -street) near 
peripheral Mass Transport stations and recommends to encourage pricing at the stations 
in such a way that park-and-ride users are segregated from general parking users by 
allowing subsidized long duration fee for park-and ride users. 
 
3. Need for Metro Parking policy 
A growing trend in parking demand is seen in the entire city, especially near the transit 
stations. Haphazard on-street parking near the Metro stations not only creates chaos in the 
adjacent residential layouts but also reduces the capacity of the arterial roads with vehicles 
parked on either sides.  
In addition to this, the lack of safe, affordable and reliable first and last mile connectivity 
options along with  poor pedestrian infrastructure  facilities discourages the Metro 
commuters in patronising M etro and  as t heir preferred mode of transport, there by 
decreasing the Public Transport patronage. 

[page 7]
 pg. 5 
In view of these concerns and in the absence of a well-organized and well-planned parking 
regime around the Metro stations, a clear established Metro Parking Policy and framework 
to manage parking has become necessary. 
Therefore, the Policy focuses to achieve the following objectives: - 
1. Creation of Park and Ride facilities for the identified Metro stations. 
2. Streamlining and enhancing the utilization of parking to ensure that the parking space 
provided by BMRCL is utilised by Metro users only. 
3.  To ensure safety and security of commuters and their vehicles at parking spaces. 
4. To reserve parking for specific user groups such as cyclists and differently abled. 
5. To determine the framework for Parking charges and Enforcement/Monitoring  
6. Effective management of parking space through the use of Smart Technology 
 
The parking policy will be reviewed periodically and the requirements will be amended 
overtime as and when first and last mile access to stations by walk, cycle, shared mobility 
or feeder buses are improved.  
OBJECTIVE 1: PARK AND RIDE FACILITY FOR IDENTIFIED METRO STATIONS 
The unprecedented growth in population and consequently in the number of vehicles is  
leading to the infamous traffic congestion in Bengaluru. Although Metro provides an efficient 
public transport alternative compared to priva te modes, private vehicle users  have largely 
refrained from shifting to Metro as their preferred travel mode. A major reason for the same 
has been identified as the severe lack of  reliable first and last mile connectivity to and from 
the Metro stations. Along with feeder bus service and good pedestrian infrastructure, the Park 
and ride system serves as an important aspect for first mile connectivity for specific station 
typologies to not only increase the Metro ridership but also to deter people from using private 
modes for long haul commute.  The following parameters will be used to determine the 
potential for park and ride service at Metro stations:  
1. Park and Ride at Peripheral/Terminal Stations/Urban Gateway 
All Peripheral/Terminal Metro Stations have to be provided with Park and Ride facilities 
since these stations cater to large catchments from suburb an areas who come to work 
in the city centre/CBD areas. Instead of driving all the way to their workplaces in the city 
centre/ CBD areas, commuters can conveniently park their vehicles at these stations and 
transfer to the Metro train/s. This approach not only alleviates traffic congestion in the 

[page 8]
 pg. 6 
urban cor e but also promotes the use of P ublic transportation, leading to a more 
efficient and sustainable commute. 
Implementation strategy 
a. BMRCL will have to compulsorily acquire /purchase about  1 to 2 acres of 
additional vacant land/s at all terminal Metro stations for building of parking 
spaces/lots. 
b. In case BMRCL is unable to acqu ire 1 to 2 acres of vacant land , BMRCL has to 
explore the possibility of providing multilevel basement parking for creating such 
parking facilities adjoining the Terminal Metro Stations. 
c. Approach State Authorities : BMRCL will approach State Government to direct 
the relevant authorities (e.g., BDA, BBMP) who own lands near Metro stations to 
transfer ownership of land to BMRCL for the development of park and ride 
facilities. 
d. Collaboration with Private entities : If adequate Government land s are not 
available, BMRCL will explore purchase or leas ing of vacant Private lands. This 
will require a detailed analysis to ensure that the land is ideally located for park-
and-ride use. This  acquisition process may include direct negotiations with 
landowners or bidding, depending on the local regulations/permitted process.  
e. Central Government assistance : BMRCL may request for financial assistance 
from Central Government in the form of VGF for land acquisition, in order to 
reduce the financial burden on BMRCL.  
f. Explore innovative finan cing models, including Land value capture  and 
congestion pricing , to help fund the development of park -and-ride facilities. 
Additionally, revenue from advertising within parking areas can offset 
operational costs, if viable and feasible. 
g. Grants and Subsidies: BMRCL will  request for grants or subsidies under 
programs aimed at reducing traffic congestion and promoting  the use of public 
transport. For ex: the FAME India Scheme  promotes the adoption of electric 
vehicles (EVs) and the development of EV charging infrastructure. It also includes 
support for eco -friendly transport initiatives, which could integrate with par k-
and-ride facilities. BMRCL may request subsidies to develop EV 
charging/swapping stations at few Metro stations for  park-and-ride facilities, 
making them more attractive for users with electric vehicles . Similarly, the 
National Smart Mobility Mission  supports projects that aim to reduce 

[page 9]
 pg. 7 
congestion, promote eco-friendly modes of transport, and integrate multi-modal 
transport. BMRCL may apply for funding to integrate park-and-ride facilities into 
a Multimodal transport system. 
 
2. Parking facilities at interchange Metro stations 
Interchange Metro Stations, which serve as critical nodes within the Metro network 
where multiple Metro lines intersect, require well -planned and efficiently managed 
parking facilities. These stations experience high passenger volumes due to their role as 
transfer hubs for commuters traveling between various lines to reach their final 
destinations. Providing adequate parking facilities at interchange Metro stations serves 
multiple purposes like Enhancing Multimodal Integration, Facilitating Seamless 
Transfers, Reducing Traffic Congestion and Pollution, significantly boost Metro ridership. 
 
Implementation Strategy 
a. Basement parking : At interchange stations, BMRCL will explore provision of 
basement parking within the station area. To achieve this, stations will have to 
be planned and designed during the initial design stage itself to incorporate 
provisions for basement parking. This may involve additional cost of construction 
of such Stations, which may have to be suitably provided for in the DPR. 
b. Over the road parking:  BMRCL has constructed stations and viaducts with minimal 
road occupancy to prioritize train movement. Where basement parking is unfeasible, 
over-the-road parking can be explored after assessment of cost/benefit analysis and 
making sure that potential bott lenecks are not created in traffic flow , using ramps or 
lifts to optimize space and meet parking demand efficiently. 
c. Integrating with Multi-level parking in the vicinity of Metro station: BMRCL will 
integrate with the nearby Multi-level parking (if availab le) and ensure the 
facilities are safe, well -lit, and equipped with smart parking solutions and plan 
for seamless connectivity between parking areas and Metro stations, with well-
designed pedestrian pathways/travelators and signage. The roads in and around 
Metro stations will be kept free for improving approach to the stations. Suitable 
directional traffic on such roads will be planned and implemented. 
a. Approach State Authorities : BMRCL will work with the State Government to 
direct agencies like BDA and BBMP to allocate Government-owned lands near 
interchange Metro stations for park-and-ride development.

BMRCL will engage with private landowners for potential acquisitions or

3.  Park-and-Ride Facilities in Residential Areas with High Population Density and 
Narrow Roads 
Metro s tations surrounded by  thickly populated residential catchment in the radius 
beyond 1 km and up  to 3 kms with no direct Metro connectivity will be equipped with 
Park and Ride facility at the Metro station to enhance accessibility and encourage use of 
the Metro system. The 1 km radius to be determined through a walk/cycle-shed instead 
of a ‘as crow flies’ distance. 
In addition, the areas with narrow r esidential roads pose significant operational 
challenges for standard 12 -meter BMTC buses, hindering effective public tr ansport 
access. In the absence of a regulated last-mile connectivity solution, commuters struggle 
to reach Metro stations. Therefore, it is essential to implement parking facilities at 
strategically located Metro stations, particularly those with a large c atchment area, to 
enhance overall accessibility and connectivity. To supplement these efforts, e-Autos can 
be encouraged to provide last mile connectivity to commuters residing in these areas. 
 
Implementation Strategy 
a. Private sector collaboration: For Metro stations facing land constraints for parking, 
BMRCL will explore Public-Private P artnerships (PPP)  with private developers, 
shopping malls, office complexes, and other commercial establishments located 
near the Metro stations  (preferably within 100m) . This could involve negotiating 
shared-use agreements  where parking spaces at these locations are utilized by 
Metro commuters during weekdays and allocated to mall and other users during 
weekends or non -peak hours. This approach optimizes existing parking  
infrastructure, alleviates land acquisition costs, and enhances the convenience for 
both Metro users and private sector stakeholders. 
b. Incentivizing Higher FAR for Commercial Establishments:  BMRCL will request the 
State Government to provide incentives in the form of higher Floor Area Ratio (FAR) 
to the private entities in the vicinity of the Metro stations allowing them to develop 
additional built-up area beyond the usual zoning limits. This additional FAR can serve 
as an incentive for private entities to collaborate with BMRCL in developing or 

[page 11]
 pg. 9 
sharing parking facilities for Metro commuters . The FAR used for parking 
infrastructure developed as part of the collaboration sh all not be counted towards 
the total FAR limit. This allows the private entities  to allocate sufficient space for 
parking facilities without compromising with their developmental potential. 
 
4. Parking at Metro Stations near major multimodal transportation Hubs 
Metro stations that are located within close vicinity to other major transportation hubs, 
such as R ailway stations, b us stands, and airports, will explore the potential for multi -
level parking facilities. Implementing such facilities can significantly enhance the 
interconnectivity between different modes of transportation, improve commuter 
convenience, and reduce congestion. 
 
Implementation Strategy 
Sharing parking space with other Transport authorities : BMRCL will collaborate with 
relevant authorities (e.g., Railways, BMTC, KSRTC, KIA) to acquire or make use of the 
underutilized Government-owned land s near Metro stations. If acquisition is not 
possible, BMRCL can jointly share the parking facilities with other agencies, allowing 
users of both the Metro and other modes of transport to park their vehicles and 
seamlessly access multimodal transportation service. 
 
5. Metro Stations with Limited thorough access  
Metro stations located near large tech parks, defence areas, lakes, airports, or other 
expensive land parcels often face significant challenges in terms of connectivity and 
accessibility. These areas typically lack adequate thoroughfare access , including 
pedestrian-friendly pathways , dedicated cycling tracks , or reliable feeder services  
such as shuttle buses or auto -rickshaw networks. As a result, commuters residing 
beyond walkable o r cyclable distances often find it difficult to access Metro stations 
conveniently. Implementing a well-planned parking facility at such stations is essential 
to address first- and last-mile connectivity challenges for commuters in these areas.  
 
Implementation Strategy 
Integrate the parking facilities with the  land owned by the  Stakeholders: Collaborate 
with stakeholders like tech parks, defence establishments, and airport authorities to 

[page 12]
 pg. 10 
identify access issues and develop joint parking spaces at the nearby locations or within 
their premises.  
 
6. Collaboration with Private entities 
For Metro stations facing land constraints for parking , BMRCL  will collaborate with 
private entities such as shopping malls , office buildings, and hotels that are located in 
the vicinity of the Metro stations and have substantial parking facilities. BMRCL will  
enter into agreement with these private players to provide shared park ing spaces for 
Metro commuters. This approach optimizes existing infrastructure and enhances 
commuter convenience. In fact, the National Urban Transport Policy (NUTP), 2006 at 
the Government of India level states that ‘ Multi-level parking complexes should be 
made a mandatory requirement in city centre(s) that have several high rise 
commercial complexes and these can come up through public-private partnerships’. 
 
Implementation Strategy 
Private sector collaboration : For Metro stations facing land constraints for parking , 
BMRCL will explore public-private partnerships (PPP) with private developers, shopping 
malls, office complexes, and other commercial establishments located near Metro 
stations. This could involve negotiating shared-use agreements where parking spaces at 
these locations are utilized by Metro commuters during  weekdays and allocated for 
malls and other users during weekends or non -peak hours. This approach optimizes 
existing parking infrastructure, alleviates land acquisition costs, and enhances the 
convenience for both Metro users and private sector stakeholders. 
 
OBJECTIVE 2:  STREAMLINING PARKING TO ENSURE THAT THE PARKING 
SPACE PROVIDED BY BMRCL IS UTILISED BY METRO USERS ONLY 
The Metro parking space  is sometimes utilised by the people who are not the actual Metro 
users/commuters but might be owning a commercial/retail outlet nearby the Metro station.  
In such cases, the non-Metro users park their vehicle/s in the Metro parking lot and the Metro 
commuter is forced to park his/her vehicle on road due to lack of parking space. However, if 
non-Metro users are pro hibited from parking, their use of private ve hicle would be 

[page 13]
 pg. 11 
disincentivised due to difficulty in finding parking space , thereby, automatically incentivising 
the use of Metro to access these areas. 
Therefore, it is important to devise provisions/ regulations to ensure that Metro parking space 
is solely utilised for Park and Ride by the Metro users/commuters only, for which: 
1. Automated Access Control Systems: Mechanisms will be developed to segregate park-
and-ride users from general parking users at these locations. This can be also done by 
integrating the Metro travel  card with the parking charges.  Such integration will also 
be facilitated through other digital ticketing means like ǪR code, FASTAG etc. 
2. The charges will  be deducted accordingly  at the time of exit of the vehicle  – normal 
charges are deducted if Metro services are used between parking in and parking out , 
and penalty charges if the card is not used for Metro travel excluding the retail shop 
owners within the Metro premises. The normal and penal charges will be as decided 
by BMRCL from time to time which will have to be duly notified/displayed prominently.  
3. This infrastructure will also ensure  
a. Well managed parking  
b. Direct relay of data to BMRCL 
c. No leakages in fare collection 
4. In addition, on-street public parking to be strictly prohibited within 250 meters of the 
Mass transit stations in accordance with the approved Bangalore Parking Policy 2.0.  
 
OBJECTIVE 3: TO ENSURE SAFETY AND SECURITY OF COMMUTERS AND 
THEIR VEHICLES AT PARKING SPACES 
Parking spaces often tend to be considered unsafe by women due to lack of activity, and eyes 
especially after dusk/late evening times . Therefore, w hile provisioning for park ing, the 
following needs to be mandated to ensure security for both  the users and the ir vehicles 
parked:  
1. All parking lots need to be under CCTV surveillance 24x7 with adequate lighting  installed 
at strategic points (Signage- Annexure 1, Figure 4 ). A storage capacity of up to 30 days 
of recording must be maintained to carry out the required audits.  
2. The CCTV recordings will be integrated to the existing CCTV coverage of station area and 
will be accessible to Metro officials only so that any abusive incident by the parking 
contractors towards the commuters can be monitored. In addition, provision will be 

[page 14]
 pg. 12 
made to stream the CCTV footage at the BMRCL central  Control Centre for the purpose 
of monitoring. 
3. Fire incident: To handle the fire related incidents at parking lots , the following facilities 
/equipment will have to be ensured:  
 Provision of sufficient fire buckets on a metal stand filled with fine and dry sand 
with water drums near the parking area  
 Provision of fire extinguishers at locations as per stipulated design standards. 
4. The emergency point of contact : The parking lots will be managed by the operators of 
the nominated agency/contractor including the matters related to the damage to or theft 
etc. of vehicles parked and resolution of any other disputes with the customers etc. 
However, in case of emergency like fire, flooding etc. affecting the safety & security of 
Metro property or commuters, the Station Controller along with station security staff will 
be called for help by Parking lot operator and also for the further coordination with civic 
authorities etc. as the case may be.  (Annexure 1, Figure 5). 
5. In addition, the parking lots needs to be well paved and sheltered to the extent possible 
in order to  avoid any kind of damage to the parked vehicles from wear and 
tear/sunlight/rainfall etc. 
6. It is also essential that the parking lot includes clear markings and designated spaces for 
cycles, two-wheelers, and four -wheelers, with prominently visible entry a nd exit signs. 
These markings should ensure smooth ingress and egress without conflicting with other 
vehicles. Ingress and egress to the parking lots should ideally be avoided from major or 
arterial roads. Wherever possible, access should be provided via roads with lighter traffic, 
such as cross streets or service roads, to prevent vehicles entering or exiting the parking 
lots from disrupting the overall traffic flow in the area. 
7. Lighting and Visibility Enhancements: Parking lots must be well illuminated to avoid any 
kind of illegal/criminal activities at night. In addition to this, the enhanced visibility makes 
the commuters especially the female commuters feel safe while dropping off/picking up 
their vehicle. 
8. Professionally trained and experienced personnel to be deployed for manning  the 
parking site  and presence of a security staff 24X7 to ensure safety and security of 
vehicles.  
9. Behaviour of the parking contractors to be monitored through CCTV by BMRCL parking  
in-charge officer and the contractor/s may be replaced immediately if found violating the 
code of conduct.

10. The parking contractors shall not charge excess amount than determined by BMRCL. In 
case of overcharging suitable penalty will be levied in addition to banning such operators 
11. The Parking Contractors/Agency shall obtain adequate Third Party Liability Insurance for 
the stipulated Sum Insured Limit and terms of BMRCL, to cover for any risk of 
damage/theft of the vehicles parked in the Parking Lot/s. 
 
OBJECTIVE 4: TO RESERVE PARKING FOR SPECIFIC USER GROUPS 
1. Parking for cycles – A minimum of 10 parking stands for cycle parking will be provided 
in each Metro station parking lots at free of cost. 
2. Parking for differently-abled 
 A minimum of one space for both two -wheeler and three wheeler parking for 
differently-abled will  be earmarked in each M etro station parking lot with 
signage. In parking lots where car parking for more than 20 cars/four-wheelers 
is provided, at least 1 car /four-wheeler parking space shall be reserved for 
differently-abled. The identified space shall be near the entry gate of the 
parking lot and / or any other convenient location within the parking lot from 
where ramp for accessing station entrance / lift is the nearest. 
 The space will be provided as per Bureau of Indian Standards, National Building 
Code of India of 2016, IRC: SP:12:2015 and Harmonized Guidelines and Space 
Standards for barrier free built environment for persons with disability and 
elderly persons, 2016 of Ministry of Urban Development, Government of India. 
 The parking spaces will be  indicated by using paint as required in above -
mentioned guidelines. The signage to be used for different modes of transport 
are shown in Annexure 1, Figure 1. 
3. Parking for BMRCL employees and official vehicles for maintenance & operation 
activities - Separate parking space will be reserved for vehicles of BMRCL 
employees/BMRCL Official vehicles at every station other than the commuter parking. 
No charges shall be levied for such vehicles of employees / O&M Vehicles. 
4. Space for pick-up and drop-off for Micro mobility modes/other modes will be located 
on cross- streets or along the service roads near the Metro station with legible signage 
(Annexure 1, Figure 6).

 pg. 14 
5. Pre-fixed Auto Rickshaw booths - Auto rickshaws  plays a crucial role in last mile 
connectivity and serv e short trips in the city of Bengaluru. BMRCL has carved out 
dedicated parking space for Pre-Fixed Auto rickshaw booths at few Metro stations and 
has planned to expand it to all the Metro stations wherein the commuters can hire 
autos from the transit station to their destination by Pre -fixing their travel cost based 
on the distance travelled.  
 
OBJECTIVE 5:  PARKING CHARGES AND ENFORCEMENT/MONITORING 
1. The BMRCL Fare chart will be displayed at the entry of the Parking lot to ensure that 
the parking C ontractors/Agencies do not charge extra for parking. The signage 
indicating the dimensions of the fare chart board will be as shown  in Annexure 1, 
Figure 2. 
2. Park-and-ride users will be charged subsidized long duration fee  for parking their 
vehicles at these facilities that nudge commuters to take transit than driving their own 
vehicles in the City. 
3. Exploring possibility of increasing parking capacity by adoption of multi -level parking 
and automated parking systems will be considered. 
4. Revision of the base parking fee to be done whenever a need is felt based on demand, 
availability of land etc., 
5. Abandonment of vehicles beyond the operational hours will be monitored through 
CCTV cameras. If a vehicle is parked for more than 3 days, the licensee / organisation 
(whoever is responsible for managing the parking space) must take adequate action to 
remove the vehicle from the parking lot by issuing notice to the vehicle owner by 
obtaining details of the ownership of the vehicle. In case of no response, a final notice 
will be issued stating that the vehicle will be auctioned.  
6. Parking of vehicles after closure of revenue services: If a commuter of Metro railway 
desires to use parking facility after revenue closure, (S)he shall share his identity such 
as address, phone number, vehicle registration details/license or any such document 
or any other document as specified by the Metro authority—to the parking in-charge, 
along with the intended duration of parking. If a vehicle remains unclaimed for more 
than thr ee days, it will be removed and auctioned  as per Motor Vehicle Act . 
Additionally, vehicles not retrieved before the end of daily revenue operations will 
incur extra charges.

of Insurance cover will be managed by the licensee. 
9. Cleanliness: The licensee shall maintain the premises clean and tidy at all times. He

OBJECTIVE 6: EFFECTIVE MANAGEMENT OF PARKING SPACE THROUGH 
USE OF SMART TECHNOLOGY 
Increasing number of vehicles at the Metro station parking lots  along with the 
mismanagement of available parking spaces lead to the parking related problems.  
For efficient management and enforcement of parking, it  is crucial to adopt technology for 
modernizing parking operations and to reduce monetary pi lferage: Few of the technological 
solutions which may be considered by Metro based on patronage of parking lot and 
economical considerations are:  
1. Mobile application: The availability of the parking slots can be displayed in the mobile 
app as well in a dis play near the Parking lot. A loop detector system needs to be 
installed in the parking lot to count the number of parking slots and indicate the slots 
available for parking. The information like availability of parking space at particular 
Metro station, will be displayed near the Parking lot as well as  integrated with the 
Namma Metro app wherein the users can search for the available parking slot at their 
selected Metro station and based on the availability they can choose to take out their 
vehicle. A robust, technology-driven parking fee collection system will be implemented 
along with a loop detector system  to eliminate pilferage and ensure accurate da ta 
recording. The system will capture details such as the number of vehicles parked, 
empty slots availab le for parking, parking duration, vehicle types, and timestamps, 
providing comprehensive records for auditing and facilitating  data-driven planning to 
improve parking services. 
2. LED display representing the vacant slots :  Every Metro station will be provided with 
graphical interface to display the real time parking availability map. The map will indicate 
vacant parking slots (cycles, two wheelers and four wheelers separately) in green colour 
and occupied parking slots in red colour at a particular  Metro station and also one

 pg. 16 
station before and after it so that the commuter can choose where to park his/her 
vehicle if his selected station does not have space for parking. 
3. Boom barrier: To avoid the revenue loss from the parking contractors, the technology 
of boom barriers may be utilised as per the space available, wherein the device will be 
installed at the entry/exit of the Parking lot and it reads the processed information 
regarding the timing and number of the vehicle, time till which the parking is done and 
delivers a receipts which then can be paid by the commuter either through Namma 
Metro app (after integrating) or any other payment gateway like 
GooglePay/PhonePe/Paytm/FASTAG etc.  
Further, the National Urban Transport Policy (NUTP), 2006 at the Government of India 
level states that ‘ All parking complexes will be encouraged to go in for  electronic 
metering so that there is better realization of parking fees to make the investments 
viable and also a better recovery of the cost of using valuable ur ban space in the 
parking of personal motor vehicles’. 
4. Central Server: A central server system is the heart of the smart parking management 
and guidance system. All the relevant data from the cameras, sensor, parking meter 
and mobile application will be collected, processed and transmitted from the central 
server to the display systems and parking meters.  All the parking related information 
of all the stations can be integrated into central parking portal which will be managed 
and monitored from one central location by the BMRCL in-charge officer.  
In addition, BMRCL will integrate its parking data with the citywide parking portal to 
enable seamless and centralized parking management. This integration will ensure a 
cohesive approach to parking operations across the City. 
5. Start-ups/operators will be encouraged to offer competitive and creative technology 
solutions that would help BMRCL to optimise the use of parking spaces.  Engaging in 
Public-private partnerships can be a strategic approach for developing and managing 
parking facilities. Such collaborations will bring in investment, expertise, and 
innovation, leading to better-managed and maintained parking space.
