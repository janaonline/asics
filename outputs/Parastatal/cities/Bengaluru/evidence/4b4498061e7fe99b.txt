Empowering women & vulnerable communities through equitable access to 
clean water supply of Cauvery scheme of 110 villages. 
Aim: 
The Cauvery Water Supply Scheme for 100 village is a strategic initiative aimed at providing 
equitable and sustainable access to safe drinking water and sanitary connections (spanning 225 sq. 
km) within the City Municipal boundary. This project will benefit approximately 300,000 
households and 1.7 million people, addressing critical issues such as systemic water scarcity, poor 
water quality, and the disproportionate burden on women and girls for water collection. 
Currently, as per the households’ survey conducted average water consumption is upto  100 liters 
per day during the summer month, with existing water sources failing to meet BIS & WHO quality 
standards. Public standposts are located between 300 meters & 1.5 kilometers from homes, 
requiring women and girls to spend 4-5 hours daily fetching water. This not only jeopardizes their 
health and limits educational opportunities but also restricts their participation in economic 
activities. 
Objective: 
The goal of this project is to enhance the reliability of water supply and sewerage services by 
augmenting water sourced from the Cauvery River. This initiative aims to improve access to safe, 
dependable water, which is crucial for empowering women to engage in economic activities such 
as farming and crafting—key drivers of financial independence. The current burden of collecting 
water often falls on women, requiring long, arduous journeys that pose safety risks and limit their 
ability to work or pursue education. This not only increases their vulnerability to violence but also 
restricts their economic opportunities. Additionally, water shortages negatively impact health and 
sanitation, particularly for pregnant women and caregivers, leading to stress, reduced productivity, 
and financial loss when family members fall ill. 
Boundary: 
The project will encompass the entire water supply system, starting from the water intake point 
and extending to each household within the 110 newly formed villages. This includes all associated 
infrastructure such as pumping stations, treatment plants, an d the distribution network up to the 
consumer delivery point.
